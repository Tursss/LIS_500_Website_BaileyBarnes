Implicit Bias Resource Page

About this guide:

The purpose of this resource guide is to provide a brief overview of Intersectionality, Implicit Biases, and Implicit Bias Testing. This short guide is designed to help bring awareness to students on how distinct social identities interact, how they shape the socio-political world around us, how we learn and unlearn them, and how they do influence human experience and behaviors.

Intersectionality:

Definition: Intersectionality is a tool intended to help understand how various axes of social division and identities interact to influence individuals and shape the sociopolitical world around us. Intersectionality aims to enhance this kind of analysis in ways that go beyond the analysis of individual axes of social division.

Why it matters: In the absence of intersectional efforts, attempts at equity and inclusion can still have the potential to exclude valuable voices and perspectives. For example, targeted efforts to uplift women in certain areas specifically might unintendedly exclude black women, transgender women, as well as women in other marginalized groups.

Implicit Biases:

Definition: Implicit biases are unconscious attitudes and perceptions that are held towards certain groups of people. These biases are learned through navigation of society and can contradict conscious stated values.

Why it matters: Because these biases manifest as automatic and unconscious responses, they silently influence important decisions that are made in the real world every day. Hiring decisions, healthcare diagnoses, judicial sentencing, and evaluations (such as in work performance, school grades, etc…) of individuals all risk being influences by these subtle but dangerous biases if left unchecked.

Implicit Bias Testing:

Definition: Implicit bias tests are behavioral assessments intended to detect and measure the degree to which an individual makes associates concepts (such as good and bad, or STEM & humanities topics) with certain social groups.

Why it matters: Testing serves as a mirror held to participants showing what cultural associations our brains have formed over time. This helps form a diagnostic baseline that can help people work towards true bias reduction via self-awareness and intentional thoughts and actions to override bad habits.
